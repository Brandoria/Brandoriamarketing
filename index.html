Visibility

Is your business invisible online? Five checks you can do in ten minutes

By the Brandoria team · 5 min read

Most owners assume customers can find them because they can find themselves. That is not a test. Here is a better one.

1. Search your business name

Open a private window so your own history does not skew the result. Type your business name. You should be the first result, with a clear description. If a directory, an old profile or a competitor shows up above you, that is your first fix.

2. Search what you sell, plus your city

Try it the way a customer would: “wedding photographer Lagos” or “accountant near me”. Look at the first page. If you are not on it, you are relying on word of mouth alone.

3. Look at your map listing

Search your name in a map app. Check the opening hours, phone number and photos. Wrong details cost real customers, and they are the quickest thing to correct.

4. Open your website on a phone

Hold your phone at arm’s length and read your homepage. Can you tell what you do within five seconds? Does the button work? Does anything need pinching and zooming?

5. Ask someone who does not know you

Give a friend your business name and ask them to find you and tell you what you do. Watch where they get stuck. That is more useful than most reports.

What to do with the answers

Fix things in this order: wrong listings, an unclear homepage, then search visibility. Marketing money spent before those are right only sends people to a page that does not work for them.

Websites

What belongs on a homepage, and what does not

By the Brandoria team · 4 min read

People decide quickly. A homepage that makes them hunt for the answer loses them. Here is what to put on it.

Put these on it

  • A headline that says what you do and who it is for. Not a slogan.
  • One main button, such as Get a quote, Book a call or Shop now. Repeat it further down the page.
  • Two or three things you offer, in plain words.
  • Proof: a real customer name, a result, or a photo of your work.
  • Your phone number or WhatsApp, visible without scrolling.

Leave these off

  • A welcome message that says nothing
  • Music or video that starts playing with sound
  • Sliders that change before anyone can read them
  • A long history of the company before you say what you sell
  • Five different buttons competing for attention

A quick test

Show the page to someone for five seconds, then hide it. Ask what the business does and what they would click. If they cannot answer both, rewrite the headline and the button first.

Keep it honest

Do not claim what you cannot back up. “Trusted by thousands” with nothing to show for it reads as empty. One real testimonial does more than ten adjectives.

Branding

Logo, brand and identity: what is the difference?

By the Brandoria team · 4 min read

The logo

A logo is the mark: a symbol, a wordmark, or both. It helps people recognise you. On its own it is the smallest part of a brand.

The brand identity

Identity is the system around the logo: colors, fonts, the style of your photos, how you write, how your documents look. It is what makes a business card, a website and a social post feel like they came from the same place.

The brand

The brand is what people think and say about you when you are not in the room. You do not fully control it. You influence it by what you deliver and how you treat people, and identity keeps that consistent.

Which one do you need first?

  • Starting with nothing: a logo and a simple identity, meaning colors, fonts and a voice.
  • Have a logo, but everything looks mismatched: an identity and a short guide.
  • Customers are confused about what you do: that is a messaging problem, not a design one. Start with one clear sentence about what you offer.

A good rule: sort out your identity before you spend on ads. Ads send people to look at you, so what they see has to hold together.

Social media

How often should a small business post on social media?

By the Brandoria team · 4 min read

Start with a rhythm you can keep

Posting every day for two weeks and then disappearing does more harm than three posts a week for a year. Pick a number you can sustain when you are busy.

A sensible starting point

  • Two or three posts a week on your main platform
  • One short video a week, if you can
  • A few minutes each day replying to comments and messages

Pick one platform first

Go where your customers already spend time. A business that sells to other businesses usually starts with LinkedIn. A shop or a creator usually starts with Instagram or TikTok. Add a second platform only when the first one runs without effort.

Make the work smaller

Write a month of posts in one sitting and schedule them. Mix the types: something useful, something that shows your work, something that shows the people behind it and, now and then, an offer. Timeless posts, such as how-tos and answers to common questions, can be repeated every few months with a new picture.

Skip these

  • Buying followers. They never buy from you.
  • Posting only offers
  • Copying a competitor’s posts

Answer every comment. The conversation counts for more than the schedule.

Websites

A launch-day checklist for your new website

By the Brandoria team · 5 min read

Content

  • Read every page out loud once. You will hear the typos.
  • Check the phone number, email and address.
  • Check every price, date and name.

Links and forms

  • Click every link and button.
  • Submit each form and confirm the message really arrives.
  • Check the WhatsApp or call button opens the right number.

Phones

  • Open the site on a real phone, small and large.
  • Make sure the text is readable without zooming.
  • Make sure the buttons are easy to tap.

Speed

Load the pages on mobile data, not just wifi. If a page takes a long time, compress the biggest images first.

Sharing

Paste your link into WhatsApp or a social post and check the preview shows the right title and picture.

The small things people forget

  • A page icon in the browser tab
  • A friendly “page not found” page
  • A privacy page if you collect names or emails
  • Analytics, so you can see what happens next
  • A saved backup of the site and your logins

One last test

Ask two people to do the main thing on the site, such as book or buy, while you watch without helping. Fix whatever they stumble on, then announce it.

How to read a marketing report without a marketing degree

By the Brandoria team · 4 min read

Reports are full of numbers that look impressive and tell you very little: impressions, reach, likes. They have their place, but they are not the point.

The four questions

  • What did you do this month?
  • What did it cost?
  • What came back, in enquiries, orders or visits that mattered?
  • What will you change next month, and why?

Numbers worth a second look

  • Enquiries or sales that came from marketing
  • Cost per enquiry: what you spent divided by what you got
  • Which posts or ads brought the most people to your site or inbox
  • What people searched for to find you

Numbers to treat with care

Likes, followers and impressions can rise while sales stay flat. They only matter if they lead somewhere, so ask what each number connects to.

Ask for plain words

Whoever runs your marketing should be able to explain the report in a short call, without jargon. If they cannot, that tells you something.
